I-94 Traffic Visualization & Analysis¶

  • I'm want to determine the indicators of heavy traffic on I-94. These indicators can be weather type, time of the day, time of the week, etc.
    • example: We may find out that the traffic is usually heavier in the summer or when it snows.

What This Is¶

This notebook showcases a practical analysis of traffic volume on I-94 to make the findings usable for scheduling, and decision-making.

Goals¶

  • Identify when and why traffic volume spikes occur (time-of-day, weekday vs weekend, seasonality)
  • Connect weather conditions to realistic expectations of congestion

How to Read This¶

Each section introduces the context, shows a chart, and summarizes implications. The goal isn't just to find correlations, but to extract usable windows for travel planning—when a small timing change can avoid heavy congestion.

Data: I-94 Interstate highway traffic¶

  • Source: https://archive.ics.uci.edu/ml/datasets/Metro+Interstate+Traffic+Volume

Column / header info¶

  • holiday Categorical US National holidays plus regional holiday, Minnesota State Fair
  • temp: Numeric Average temp in kelvin
  • rain_1h: Numeric Amount in mm of rain that occurred in the hour
  • snow_1h: Numeric Amount in mm of snow that occurred in the hour
  • clouds_all: Numeric Percentage of cloud cover
  • weather_main: Categorical Short textual description of the current weather
  • weather_description: Categorical Longer textual description of the current weather
  • date_time: DateTime Hour of the data collected in local CST time
  • traffic_volume: Numeric Hourly I-94 ATR 301 reported westbound traffic volume

How We'll Use It¶

  • Convert date_time to usable components (hour, month, weekday) to isolate daily and weekly patterns
  • Split by day vs night to expose distinct distributions (commute vs off-hours)
  • Group by month and weather to explore seasonality and conditions that correlate with congestion

Split: Day vs Night Traffic¶

We define day traffic as 07:00–18:59 and night traffic as 19:00–06:59. This aligns with practical commute behavior patterns and exposes differences in how volumes cluster.

Expectation:

  • Night traffic should be lower and more tightly clustered.
  • Day traffic should show higher variance, including commute spikes.
is_day_traffic = (traffic['date_time'].dt.hour >= 7) & (traffic['date_time'].dt.hour < 19)
is_night_traffic = (traffic['date_time'].dt.hour < 7) | (19 <= traffic['date_time'].dt.hour)

plt.figure(figsize=(10,3))

impressions¶

  • Day traffic averages ~4k with an spike at 5k.

Use Case¶

  • If you can shift departures into night hours, you reliably avoid the heaviest congestion.
  • If you must travel during the day, aim for windows outside of typical rush hours; we’ll isolate those next.

  • Planning against these conditions—like avoiding high-risk late afternoon slots—can meaningfully reduce travel time variability.

Insights¶

  • Time
    • Traffic is heavier on weekdays
    • Traffic Volume goes down during the winter months
    • on Business Days, Traffic is the worst around 7 & 5
  • Weather: Traffic is worst(in order) during:
    • shower snow
    • light shower and snow
    • proximity thunderstorm with rain

Key Findings¶

  • Day vs Night split exposes two distinct regimes; night travel reliably reduces congestion.
  • Weekday commute windows (AM and PM) are tight and steep—small timing shifts pay off.
  • Seasonal trends matter (lower in winter), but disruptive weather conditions are the practical signal for risk and variability.
